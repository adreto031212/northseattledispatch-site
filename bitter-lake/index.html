The latest in Bitter Lake

November 12, 2026

West of the freeway, SDOT has finished early design for N 130th Street from Palatine Ave N to the I-5 overpass, plus Greenwood Ave N from N 130th to N 134th. The design has a protected bike lane each way, pavement rebuilt for buses and freight, new crossings, relocated bus stops, street trees and drainage work. Construction is as soon as 2028, and the team takes comments at N130st_BitterLake_HallerLake_Project@seattle.gov. SDOT SOLD:Bitter Lake

November 5, 2026

Bitter Lake Playfield, 13035 Linden Ave N, came out of its renovation in 2025 with a new play area, three accessible restrooms, new picnic tables, and tennis court lights upgraded to use less energy and cut glare. It also has lighted ball fields with bleachers, a wading pool and a short stretch of the lake's shoreline. Seattle Parks

October 29, 2026

Bitter Lake Playfield, 13035 Linden Ave N, came out of its renovation in 2025 with a new play area, three accessible restrooms, new picnic tables, and tennis court lights upgraded to use less energy and cut glare. It also has lighted ball fields with bleachers, a wading pool and a short stretch of the lake's shoreline. Seattle Parks

October 22, 2026

SPU's contractor is finishing demolition of the empty Bitter Lake reservoir, southwest of Linden Ave N and N 143rd Street, scheduled for June through October, with two temporary access roads off Linden. The new reservoir holds the same 21 million gallons, partly buried on a smaller footprint; it goes in from 2027 to mid-2028, and the project runs to mid-2029 with more park space and public art. SPU

October 17, 2026

Bitter Lake Community Center, 13035 Linden Ave N, holds its free Big Pumpkin Bash on Saturday, October 24, 6 to 8 PM. Bitter Lake is also the western end of Metro's new Route 77 to Pinehurst Station and Lake City. Seattle Parks Halloween events · Bitter Lake Community Center

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a North Seattle newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in North Seattle that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from North Seattle's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
