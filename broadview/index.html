The latest in Broadview

November 12, 2026

The Broadview Branch of the Seattle Public Library, 12755 Greenwood Ave N, was designed with Native American longhouse architecture in mind, and Marvin Oliver of the Haida nation made art for the building. It is open Monday to Saturday, until 8 PM Tuesday through Thursday, closed Sunday, and takes book returns around the clock. Seattle Public Library SOLD:Broadview

November 5, 2026

Llandover Woods Greenspace, 14499 3rd Ave NW, has several gravel trails through dense northwest forest, and one trail opens a narrow view of the Olympics. The park is open 4 AM to 11:30 PM. Seattle Parks

October 29, 2026

Llandover Woods Greenspace, 14499 3rd Ave NW, is a few gravel trails through dense forest, quiet enough, the city says, to hear a leaf drop. One trail opens a narrow view of the Olympics. Open 4 AM to 11:30 PM. Seattle Parks

October 22, 2026

Beyond the salmon, Carkeek Park keeps two quieter draws: Piper's Orchard, heritage fruit trees on the eastern slope of the Piper's Creek Trail near the Eddie McAbee entrance, and the demonstration gardens just right of the main entrance, the largest in King County, kept up over 64 work parties a year. Carkeek Park

October 17, 2026

The chum salmon are back in Piper's Creek. Carkeek Park's free Creekside Salmon Journey Days run Saturdays and Sundays from October 24 to December 6, 10:30 AM to 3:30 PM, and the Salmon Celebration is Saturday, November 21, same hours. This morning, October 17, a guided "Color Your Fall" walk runs 10 AM to noon ($10 for ages 4 to 17 and 50 and over, $20 for 18 to 49). The park is 216 acres with 6 miles of trails, at 950 NW Carkeek Park Rd. Seattle Parks Halloween and fall events · Carkeek Park

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a North Seattle newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in North Seattle that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from North Seattle's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
